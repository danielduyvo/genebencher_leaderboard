Which AI agents can do real omics work?

GeneBencher scores relatively low cost AI agents and models on complete bioinformatics, omics and statistical-genetics analyses. Every result is tied to a dataset checksum, a scoring rubric and a published protocol. No prompts, inputs or truth data are public.

Final 26 of 26 planned tasks 60 min cap 6 ranked models

Read the caveats before comparing scores.

Six models on all 26 tasks with a 60-minute cap per task. All six completed every task and are ranked. One seed per task.

Scope of the field: a budget-limited sample of relatively low-cost models, not a survey of the strongest models available.

What limits these numbers
  • Model coverage is a budget-limited convenience sample, not a survey of the strongest available models. The six ranked runs are relatively low-cost entries, mostly Flash/Free tiers and free previews; no lab's flagship frontier model was run. The field is limited to what could be funded from one shared weekly OpenCode Go allowance. Gemini 4 Argon, Grok 4.7 and Kimi K3.1 Pro were dropped before testing because each could consume most of that allowance in a few tasks. The benchmark is also still early — this is its first full edition, and the task set, rubrics and protocol are being refined. Treat the ranking as a preliminary comparison of affordable models, not as evidence about the best available model.
  • Hy4, MiniMax M3.1 Flash and GLM-5.3 Flash are not published. The shared weekly Go allowance ran out before they could attempt most tasks, so their partial scores would not be a fair comparison.
  • LongCat 2.5 Free is not published either. Its provider rate limited 24 of 26 tasks, so only two annotation tasks were measured. A 0.831 average over those two tasks would misrepresent the model, and the other 24 tasks were never attempted.
  • The 60-minute wall-clock cap is part of the protocol. A task that does not finish in time is a failure in the strict score; slow but correct approaches can be cut off.
  • A task score measures the rubric's deliverables, not how hard the analysis is to code. Colocalization (FM-02) is simple to implement but averaged 0.377 across the ranked models, because the rubric scores calibrated statistical output rather than whether the pipeline runs.
  • One seed per task. Replicate variance is not estimated.
  • The comparison confounds model capability with agent-harness behaviour. Gemini 3.8 Flash runs through agy, the other models through OpenCode.
  • The local subprocess adapter was used instead of the container backend.
  • Free OpenCode Go models cost no allowance; paid models draw on one shared account-wide weekly budget, which is why the run was paused and resumed across two weeks.
